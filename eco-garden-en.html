Typhoon-resistance mechanics

8 tension cables tie the dome and the base into one super-rigid body

Why it cannot be tipped

A floating house is not "one big pontoon" but a three-stage closed loop:

① Tie　8 tension cables (R 0.100 m, ring radius 10.93 m) tie the red dome (27.000 m base) to the bottom grid platform (29.2 × 29.1 m). The cables carry only tension; the two ends react against each other, forming a pre-stressed self-balanced system.

② Ballast　Very low ballast (centre of gravity z ≈ −2.2 m; overall bounding box Z ∈ [−2.450, 6.600] m) supplies the restoring moment, while the internal tension loop dissipates disturbances inside the structure.

③ Rigidity　Together these make the house a all-round anti-capsize matrix — it neither comes apart in a typhoon nor heels over in a surge.

Crucially, it needs no seabed piles — anchoring is self-anchored with 0.25 m embedment, which is precisely why the whole port can be relocated.
